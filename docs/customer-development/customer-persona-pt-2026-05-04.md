## Problem Context

Independent physical therapy practices face the same administrative collapse as chiropractic and dermatology, with one compounding factor: PT care is inherently longitudinal. A patient who quits after 3 visits instead of 12 loses the clinical outcome *and* destroys the economics of the treatment plan. The combination of insurance visit limits, poor patient compliance with home exercise programs, and billing complexity creates a trifecta of revenue leakage that solo PT owners are uniquely ill-equipped to handle.

## Segment C — Independent Physical Therapist (Owner-Operator)

**Profile**
- Solo or small group clinic (1–5 providers, typically 1–3)
- Mix of insurance (health, workers' comp, auto/PIP) + cash-pay
- High visit volume: 10–30 patients/day per PT
- Staff: 0–2 PTAs (physical therapy assistants), front desk, often no dedicated biller
- Tight margins: overhead 55–70% of collections (similar to chiro)
- Often compete against hospital-employed PT departments, ATI Physical Therapy, Select Medical, and other large chains

**Job-To-Be-Done**
> "Keep my patients on their plans, get paid for the work I do, and run a practice that doesn't eat me alive after hours."

**Core Pain Points** *(ranked by frequency in online discourse)*
1. **Patient compliance / adherence drop-off** — patients complete 3–5 visits, feel marginally better, and self-discharge before completing the care plan; PTs lose 40–60% of planned treatment revenue per episode; home exercise program (HEP) non-compliance compounds this — patients don't do the work between sessions and plateau
2. **Insurance authorization burden** — PT visits require prior auth at many payers; visit limits (often 20–30/year across all PT) force re-authorization mid-episode; medical necessity documentation is time-consuming and frequently denied at first pass
3. **Billing complexity & claim denials** — PT billing codes (CPT 97xxx series) have specific documentation requirements; workers' comp and PIP billing have different rules per state; denial rates of 10–20% common at small practices
4. **EHR / documentation burden** — SOAP notes, functional outcomes (LEFS, DASH, PSFS), progress notes for insurance — all manual or fragmented; PT-specific EHRs (WebPT, Therabill, Clinicient) are expensive or poorly designed for small clinics
5. **Competing with hospital systems and chains** — ATI, Select, hospital-employed PT departments have marketing budgets, brand recognition, and insurance contracts that small independents can't match; independent PTs must win on relationship, outcomes, and availability

**Willingness to Pay**
- Moderate-High — average independent PT clinic bills $300K–$700K/year; will pay $150–$400/month for tools that reduce drop-off or recover billing leakage
- Decision trigger: clear demonstration that tool recovers 1–2 treatment episodes per month (= $400–$1,200 recovered revenue)

**Search Behavior**
- LinkedIn: professional updates, APTA and state PT association groups, practice management discussions
- Reddit: r/physicaltherapy (clinical + business), r/physicaltherapists, r/PTschool (pre-owner PTs)
- X/Twitter: #PTTwitter, #PhysicalTherapy, #PrivatePracticePT
- Facebook Groups: Physical Therapy Owners Club (PTOC — very active private group), Private Practice Section (APTA-affiliated)
- Preferred contact: Facebook group, state APTA chapter events, warm intro from peer PT

## Wedge Hypothesis for PT

> **Patient adherence + treatment plan completion** — this is where independent PTs lose the most revenue. Unlike chiro (where the billing wedge is equally strong), PT's most acute expressed pain in online communities is around patients not completing their plan of care. The problem is:
> - Emotionally resonant for the clinician ("I know this patient needs 12 visits but they quit at 4")
> - Quantifiable revenue impact (~$400–$1,200 per dropout episode)
> - Software-solvable via automated HEP follow-up, progress check-ins, re-engagement messaging

## Qualification Criteria for PT Prospects

A PT prospect is **qualified** if they meet ALL of:
1. **Role match**: Physical Therapist (DPT/PT) in independent/private practice — NOT hospital-employed or chain clinic (ATI, Select, Athletico)
2. **Pain evidence**: Has publicly expressed at least one of: compliance drop-off, prior auth burden, billing complexity, EHR frustration, admin overwhelm
3. **Reachability**: Has a contactable profile (LinkedIn URL, Reddit username, Twitter handle)
4. **Geography**: Seattle-area for design partner tier (Score 9–10); US-wide for pipeline (Score 7–8)
