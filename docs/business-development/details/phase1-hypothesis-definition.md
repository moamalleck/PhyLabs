# Phase 1: Hypothesis Definition
**Date:** 2026-04-27
**Job:** business-idea-validation-and-scoping
**Project:** PhysicianLabs

---

## Founder's Core Spark

Healthcare providers don't know how to launch or run clinics — medical school didn't teach them this, and they don't want to do it. But it matters because their revenue and monthly profitability depend on it.

**PhysicianLabs** is the Clinical Operating System that automates three things for healthcare practices and clinic-owner doctors:
1. Top-of-funnel patient intake
2. Patient engagement and retention
3. Clinic revenue cycle management (RCM)

**Why Now:** Healthcare is at a breaking point — doctors are burning out, clinic margins are shrinking, and patient outcomes are declining. A bottom-up solution is needed.

---

## Value Hypotheses (All Five)

| ID | Hypothesis | Riskiest Assumption |
|----|-----------|---------------------|
| H1 | Overwhelmed clinic owners will pay for an automated OS because their time is better spent on clinical care | They'll trust software over a practice manager or billing service |
| H2 | Physicians about to launch a clinic have no structured operational path and will adopt an all-in-one platform from day one | They make the buying decision before revenue exists |
| H3 | Established clinic owners lose 15–30% of potential revenue due to poor RCM and will pay to recover it — ROI is the primary purchase driver | They can quantify their own revenue leakage today |
| H4 | Clinics lose significant recurring revenue from patient no-shows/drop-off, and automated engagement is the highest-leverage problem | Retention has a bigger revenue impact than new patient acquisition |
| H5 | HIPAA complexity creates a moat — clinic owners will pay a premium for compliance-ready infrastructure | Compliance anxiety is a purchase trigger, not just a nice-to-have |

---

## Systemic Riskiest Assumptions

| # | Assumption | Why It's Risky |
|---|-----------|----------------|
| A1 | Doctors will adopt bottom-up software without hospital/EHR system approval | Epic/EMR lock-in is real |
| A2 | Independent clinics have budget and authority to buy SaaS | Many are cash-constrained post-launch |
| A3 | Regulatory environment permits a third-party infrastructure layer for billing + intake without special licenses | Could require clearinghouse or other licenses |
| A4 | Physicians will trust AI/automation in clinical-adjacent workflows | High trust bar in healthcare |

---

## Selected Hypotheses for Validation

**Primary (validate first):**
- **H3** — Revenue Leakage / RCM (clearest ROI signal, easiest to falsify)
- **H2** — Pre-Launch Physician (greenfield segment, no incumbent loyalty)

**Secondary (validate after):**
- **H1** — Overwhelmed Clinic Owner

## Ideal Customer Profile (First Pass)

| Dimension | Profile |
|-----------|---------|
| **Role** | Clinic owner (GP or specialist), solo or small group (1–5 providers) |
| **Stage** | Pre-launch (0–6 months to opening) OR early-stage (1–3 years in, struggling with ops) |
| **Geography** | US-based (HIPAA jurisdiction, fee-for-service billing model) |
| **Pain trigger** | Revenue declining OR just hired first admin OR overwhelmed by billing rejections |
| **Budget signal** | Already paying a practice manager, billing service, or EMR |
