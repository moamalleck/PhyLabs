## The Non-Negotiable Questions (Mom Test compliant)

Past-focused and behavioral. No "would you pay" or "would you use" hypotheticals (counterfactual WTP is not valid evidence). Do not pitch until the commitment ask near the end. Ask, then stop talking.

**For Tamiko (operator vantage, highest value):**

1. "When a new physician joined the group, or when the business side was set up or restructured, which parts consistently went wrong or took far longer than anyone expected?"
2. "Of all the setup and financial work you owned, what did the practice actually pay an outside person to handle, and what did you wish you'd had help with but never found?"
3. "Looking back, where did the practice leave money on the table or pay for a mistake because something wasn't structured right from the start?"

**For Beverlie (physician vantage + ICP path):**

4. "When you joined an independent group instead of taking a hospital job, what about the business and financial side did you have to figure out that your training never prepared you for?"
5. "Who do you know right now who's a year or two from going independent, or just did, and is in the thick of figuring this out?" (the referral / ICP-access question; the most important single question against the investor bar)

**Commitment ask (design partner, near the end, to Tamiko specifically):**

6. "I'm building the operating system for launching and financially structuring an independent practice. You've run exactly that from the inside. Would you be willing to look at what I'm building every couple of weeks and tell me where I'm wrong?"

## Mom Test Guardrails (taped to the top of your screen)

- Talk about their past and their life, not your idea.
- Never ask "would you pay" or "would you use this." Ask what they already did, hired, or spent.
- Compliments and "that's a great idea" are not data. Specifics, money, and emotion are data.
- When they say something charged ("it was brutal," "I had no idea," "we almost..."), that emotional texture is confirming evidence. Write the exact words down.
- After you ask a question, stop talking. Silence pulls out the real answer.
- You are not there to defend the idea. You are there to find out if it is real.
