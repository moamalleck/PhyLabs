## ICP Fit Assessment

Hannan is a **partial ICP** -- closer than Shaun Cowan but not a clean Launch Gauntlet fit. He joined a PE-owned practice directly after fellowship and never went through an independent clinic setup. He did not experience the information vacuum of the Launch Gauntlet.

However, he sits at an active fork that is adjacent to the ICP:

- He has been offered partnership but the economics are unattractive
- He is considering moving to another practice
- Going independent is implicitly on the table as an option

A physician in this position -- evaluating a diluted PE partnership vs. other paths -- is one conversation away from the Launch Gauntlet ICP. He is a useful source for the patient acquisition and practice operations pain clusters, and a potential referral source for physicians who are closer to the independent-launch decision.

## New Signals (Not Previously Captured)

### 1. Patient acquisition channels named precisely

Hannan identified the three channels that actually drive new patients in a competitive urban ENT market:

| Channel | Mechanic |
|---------|---------|
| Google search / SEO | Being a top result when patients search the specialty in their area |
| Online reviews | Feed directly into search ranking and patient trust signals |
| Insurance network directories | Patients filter by insurer, location, and availability; high-ranking in-network providers are selected first |

He ranked intake as the most important of the three product areas (intake, engagement, RCM) specifically because Atlanta has high practice density. In a competitive market, getting patients through the door is the primary bottleneck before engagement or billing even become relevant.

**Impact:** These three channels should be named explicitly in any product specification or pitch related to the patient acquisition module. They are not generic -- they are what physicians in competitive US metro areas already use and understand.

---

### 2. Procedure-level patient mix matters, not just volume

Hannan made a precision observation: within ENT, some procedures (earwax cleaning, tinnitus with no treatment options) generate one-time visits with no recurring value. He does not want to fill his clinic with them. He wants patients who have conditions requiring ongoing or high-value care.

This generalises across all target specialties:
- **Derm:** one-time mole checks vs. biologic-dependent psoriasis patients (recurring, high-margin)
- **PT:** single triage sessions vs. 12-session treatment plans
- **Chiro:** one-time acute visits vs. maintenance care patients

**Impact:** The patient pipeline module should not optimise for raw patient volume. It should help physicians understand and improve their patient mix -- the proportion of high-value, recurring patients relative to one-time visits. This is a more sophisticated and more useful framing than "get more patients."

---

### 3. PE-owned practice dynamics: a distinct and growing adjacent segment

Hannan's practice structure reveals a specific economic squeeze not yet documented: founding physicians sold the practice to PE, became shareholders in the PE entity, and now earn through dividends. New joiners like Hannan are associates on a formal partner track, but the equity they are offered is diluted by the PE layer before it reaches them.

The result: physicians who have built patient relationships and clinical reputation over 2-3 years are offered partnership economics that don't reflect that contribution. The partnership offer has "not a lot of bite to it."

This is a distinct segment from both hospital-employed physicians (no ownership path) and independent practice owners (full equity retained). PE-backed associate physicians are:
- Earning clinical revenue that partially flows to PE rather than to them
- On a partnership track with diluted upside
- Actively evaluating other options

This segment is likely to grow as PE consolidation of independent practices continues. The TAM risk identified in the business validation report (independent physician ownership declining) is partly explained by this dynamic: physicians are not just joining hospitals -- they are joining PE-backed groups that are systematically extracting the ownership upside while retaining the physician's clinical production.

**Impact:** Worth tracking as a downstream segment. These physicians are one bad partnership offer away from the independent-launch decision. They are also a potential channel for reaching physicians who are actively reconsidering their practice structure.

---

### 4. Hidden job market for physicians in competitive geographies

Hannan described a parallel to the hidden job market that exists in professional services: some practices make offers directly to physicians they know through network connections before advertising a position publicly. In competitive or desirable geographies, practices with known near-term hiring needs start conversations with candidates 2-3 years in advance.

This extends the ICP recognition signal further: a physician who is known to be high-performing and approaching the end of training in a desirable market may be contacted by practices before they have made any visible decision. The trigger for reaching them is not their announcement -- it is their proximity to a decision window that practices can predict based on training timelines.
