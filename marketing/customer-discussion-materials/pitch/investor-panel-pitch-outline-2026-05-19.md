## Framework Comparison: Why Problem/Solution Is the Right Choice

| Framework | Best Used When | Advantage | PhysicianLabs Fit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Problem/Solution** | Real customer problem backed by data exists; pain is documentable and expensive | Anchors the entire narrative in a verified, dollar-quantified problem the investor immediately believes | BEST FIT: $280K mistake is a documented, verifiable story; 84% vs. 42% stat is MGMA-sourced; three pain points have dollar ranges |
| **Vision/Opportunity** | Moonshot market or brand-new category where "Why Now" timing is the lead argument | Inspires; works when the market doesn't know it has a problem yet | WEAK FIT: The problem already exists and is already being solved by incumbents — leading with vision invites "why not Physicians Thrive?" before you've established the gap |
| **Team** | Founding team has exceptional, rare domain expertise that makes the idea fundable on credentials alone | Transfers investor confidence to the founders before the market case is made | SECONDARY ROLE: McKinsey + Amazon + physician family is strong, but the problem proof is stronger as the lead; Team confirms the narrative, not the other way around |
| **Traction** | Company already has exceptional growth metrics or paying customers | Removes the "will anyone pay?" question before it's asked | PREMATURE: No paying customers yet; leading with traction at pre-revenue invites questions before the problem is established |

**Conclusion:** Problem/Solution leads with the pain, quantifies it, proves it is documentable, then positions PhysicianLabs as the only productized answer. The team slide confirms founder-market fit. Traction becomes a milestone claim ("first paid engagement in 30 days") rather than a current-state embarrassment.

### 2. PROBLEM

The hook, the systemic pattern, and the proof point. The problem slide must make the investor feel the pain before you show the solution.

**The hook story:**
- A physician in Washington State incorporated her physical therapy practice at the wrong time
- That single decision cost her $280,000
- Her accountant found it 18 months after she opened
- It was completely preventable

**The three documented, preventable mistakes:**
- **Incorporation timing:** The wrong entity structure formed at the wrong time costs $200,000 to $400,000 in preventable tax losses over five years. Most physicians discover this 18 months post-launch — too late to reverse.
- **Credentialing sequence error:** A 90-day payer enrollment delay costs $100,000 to $450,000 in lost revenue during the months a physician is open but not reimbursed. The sequence is fixable before launch only.
- **Working capital gap:** 92% of new independent clinics hit a cash crisis at months two to four. The overhead is fixed; the revenue is delayed. The gap is predictable and financeable — but only if planned before opening.

**Why this happens:**
- Medical school trains physicians to practice medicine; it provides zero business formation training
- The AMA has documented that most physicians going independent have no formal guidance on entity structure, credentialing sequence, or working capital planning
- The $30,000 to $60,000 consulting firms that solve this problem exist — but they are ad-hoc, relationship-dependent, and accessible only to physicians who already know the right people

**The proof point (anchors the whole pitch):**
- Physicians who engage an external advisor launch on time and on budget at **84%**
- Physicians who go it alone: **42%**
- Source: MGMA (Medical Group Management Association), 2023
- That 42-point gap is the entire business case

### 3. SOLUTION

What PhysicianLabs is, how it works, and why the TurboTax analogy is accurate.

**The category claim:**
- First fixed-curriculum, fixed-price pre-launch advisory program for independent physicians
- Not a consulting firm. Not a generic SaaS. A productized curriculum.

**The TurboTax analogy (explain it, don't just say it):**
- TurboTax did not replace accountants — it productized the knowledge accountants hold
- A local accountant can do your taxes; so can TurboTax. The difference: structured, sequential, accessible to anyone, at a fixed price
- PhysicianLabs does the same: the $45,000 consulting firm knows exactly which entity structure, which credentialing sequence, which working capital buffer. PhysicianLabs delivers that same knowledge in a structured 12-week program at a fixed price

**Why it works:**
- The knowledge already exists — proven by the 84% outcome when physicians engage an advisor
- The gap is access and structure, not knowledge creation
- A fixed curriculum means every physician gets the same high-quality guidance regardless of who they know

**The three tiers:**
- $500 Launch Kit: 50-step self-serve digital toolkit. Every checklist, template, and framework in the right sequence.
- $2,500 Launch Program: 12-week founder-guided program with specialist introductions timed to checklist stage
- $10,000 Full Launch: White-glove done-for-you with hands-on coordination at every decision point
- All tiers include $200/month ongoing advisory subscription post-launch

**Year 1 delivery model:**
- Concierge: founder-delivered. This is intentional — the first 10 engagements build the specialist referral network and generate the product specification data for Year 2 SaaS.

### 4. PRODUCT

What exists today vs. what is planned. Be precise and honest about the current state.

**What exists today:**
- The founder (advisory delivery)
- A 50-step pre-launch checklist (structured and sequenced)
- A 12-week program framework
- A vetted specialist network being built engagement by engagement (healthcare CPAs, attorneys, credentialing consultants)
- Delivery method: Zoom + structured documents

**The "product" is the program, not a SaaS (Year 1):**
- This is the concierge-first strategy — a deliberate choice, not a limitation
- Each engagement identifies which of the 50 steps generates the most physician anxiety
- Each engagement adds one specialist relationship to the referral network
- After 10 engagements, the product specification for the SaaS is built from real usage data, not assumptions

**Year 2 SaaS product (planned):**
- Web application replacing the Zoom/document delivery with a guided digital program
- Each checklist step linked to templates, timing guidance, and specialist introductions
- Physician dashboard showing completion status and upcoming deadlines
- Architecture: HIPAA-compliant by design; zero PHI stored (advisory content only)

**Year 3 marketplace (planned):**
- Two-sided marketplace: physicians (demand side) + vetted specialists (supply side)
- Specialists listed by category, geography, and specialty
- Matching algorithm based on physician profile and checklist stage
- Network effect: every new physician engagement adds specialist demand; every specialist relationship adds physician supply

**Why concierge-first is the right product strategy:**
- "Do Things That Don't Scale" (Paul Graham) — the concierge phase generates the data that makes the SaaS precise rather than generic
- A large incumbent can copy a checklist; they cannot copy a specialist network built relationship by relationship over 18 months

### 6. BUSINESS MODEL

Unit economics, revenue projections, and the three-stage evolution.

**Three tiers:**
- Launch Kit: $500 one-time
- Launch Program: $2,500 + $200/month ongoing
- Full Launch: $10,000 + $200/month ongoing

**Unit economics (Year 1 projections):**
- Blended ARPU: $4,600 per physician
- Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC): $200 (zero paid — organic through physician communities and referrals)
- LTV:CAC ratio: 23.5:1
- Gross margin: greater than 80% (delivery is founder time + specialist coordination)
- Revenue Year 1: $198,000 (65 physicians at blended ARPU of $4,600 minus cost of delivery)

**Revenue projections:**
- Year 1: $198,000 — 65 physicians, zero paid CAC, concierge delivery
- Year 2: $600,000 to $760,000 — concierge + early SaaS subscriptions
- Year 3: $1.8M to $2.5M — SaaS + marketplace transaction fees

**Three-stage evolution:**
- Stage 1 (Year 1): Concierge validates. Founder delivers the program and builds the specialist network. First 10 engagements produce the SaaS product specification.
- Stage 2 (Year 1-2): SaaS scales. Program delivery moves to a web platform. Marginal cost per physician drops toward zero.
- Stage 3 (Year 2+): Marketplace compounds. Specialist network becomes a two-sided marketplace. Network effects create a moat that cannot be replicated quickly by a large incumbent.

**Why the LTV:CAC is real:**
- $200 CAC at zero paid spend reflects real acquisition cost — organic discovery through physician communities, warm referrals, content
- As SaaS launches, CAC will rise with paid channels; LTV rises faster as the $200/month subscription becomes the primary revenue driver

### 7. MARKET

TAM/SAM/SOM with the headwind acknowledged before the tailwind is claimed.

**The market today (wedge TAM):**
- 5,000 to 10,000 new independent physician practice formations per year in the United States (AMA data)
- Each formation represents $30,000 to $60,000 currently spent on unproductized consulting
- New-launch advisory TAM: $23M to $46M annually

**The headwind (say this before an investor does):**
- Independent physician ownership dropped from 60.1% in 2012 to 42.2% in 2024 (AMA Physician Practice Benchmark Survey 2024)
- The conventional narrative: independent practice is dying
- This is the incomplete version

**The tailwind (and why the headwind makes it stronger):**
- The physicians choosing independence today are doing it deliberately — not by default
- Direct Primary Care market: $61.34 billion, growing at 10 to 15% annually (CAGR)
- 9 new physician-ownership platforms launched in 2025 specifically to enable independent practice
- The era of physicians accidentally going independent is over; the era of intentional independence is beginning
- Intentional independence means higher willingness to pay for expert guidance, not lower

**The expansion TAM (what turns this into a fund-returnable thesis):**
- 120,000+ existing independent practices in the United States
- Ongoing advisory subscription at $200/month: $290M/year
- This is the number that makes a $23M-$46M wedge TAM relevant to a VC fund
- The ongoing subscription TAM is an order of magnitude larger than the new-launch TAM

### 8. COMPETITION

Accurate competitive framing. The correct claim is "first productized competitor" — not "no direct competitors."

**The existing unproductized market:**
- Physicians Thrive — financial planning and advisory for physicians
- Independent Practice Partners — practice startup consulting
- TruMediq — physician practice launch advisory
- Cornerstone Healthcare Consulting — multi-state practice formation consulting
- Physician Practice Specialists — credentialing and startup advisory
- These firms charge $30,000 to $60,000 per engagement, ad-hoc and relationship-dependent

**What incumbents do right:**
- Deep domain expertise
- Access to vetted specialists
- Proven track record (these firms produce the 84% outcome)

**What incumbents do wrong:**
- Ad-hoc delivery: every engagement is different, no repeatable curriculum
- Relationship-dependent: only accessible to physicians who already know the right people
- No fixed price: clients cannot predict total cost
- No productization path: the knowledge stays locked inside individual consultants

**The PhysicianLabs differentiation:**
- First productized competitor: fixed curriculum, fixed price, accessible to any physician regardless of referral network
- Not claiming to be first to serve this market — claiming to be first to make the service accessible and scalable

**Competitive moat (the answer to "why can't Physicians Thrive copy this tomorrow?"):**
- They can copy the checklist content
- They cannot copy the specialist referral network — built relationship by relationship through each engagement, taking 18 to 24 months minimum
- They cannot copy the structured product experience that makes the knowledge accessible at $500 to $10,000
- Large incumbent organizations move slowly; a 12-month head start on the specialist network is a durable advantage

- Inbound: website design partner application driving organic discovery

**30-day sprint (the immediate GTM plan):**
- Week 1-2: Request 3 warm referrals from the physician network; schedule discovery calls on Mom Test discipline
- Week 3: First paid engagement target close
- Week 4: Second and third discovery conversations; refine the onboarding process

**Why zero paid CAC is the right Year 1 strategy:**
- The independent physician community trusts peer referrals and community recommendations above advertising
- Paid acquisition before product-market fit is waste — the concierge phase requires a small number of committed customers, not volume
- Precision outreach (personalized DMs, warm intros, community trust-building) converts faster than broadcast for a 3-to-6 customer sprint

**Year 2 GTM evolution (as SaaS launches):**
- Content marketing: SEO-optimized educational content around "how to start a medical practice," "physician practice formation," "payer credentialing sequence"
- Paid search: target physicians actively searching for practice formation guidance
- Specialist channel: reciprocal referrals from healthcare CPAs and attorneys who refer physicians to PhysicianLabs as part of the specialist network

**Year 3 GTM evolution (as marketplace matures):**
- Specialists become a distribution channel: every vetted specialist in the network is an organic referral source
- Network effects reduce CAC as the specialist network grows — each new specialist adds physician referral capacity

## Framework Comparison Summary (Reference)

**Why Problem/Solution works for PhysicianLabs specifically:**

1. The problem is real, documentable, and dollar-quantified — the $280,000 Washington State story is specific and verifiable. No other framework leads as cleanly with a story that concrete.

2. The 84% vs. 42% stat from MGMA is investor-grade proof that the problem has a known solution — it just isn't accessible. This transforms the pitch from "we think physicians need help" to "we know what help produces and who currently provides it."

3. The Team and Traction elements support the Problem/Solution narrative rather than replacing it. McKinsey + Amazon + physician family confirms the founder is the right person to build the solution. The 30-day milestone turns the traction gap into a demonstration of methodology.

4. The Vision/Opportunity and Traction frameworks would each require the pitch to lead with claims PhysicianLabs cannot yet prove. Problem/Solution leads with data that already exists.
