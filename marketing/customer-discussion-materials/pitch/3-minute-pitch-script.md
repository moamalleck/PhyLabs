**Format:** Word-for-word delivery script, Demo Day / Startup Accelerator

**Audience:** Startup accelerator mentors (VCs, founders, accelerator operators)

## SLIDE 1 — Hook + Problem (0:00–0:45)

*[Advance to slide 1. Pause. Look at the audience before speaking.]*

"A physician in Washington State incorporated her physical therapy practice at the wrong time.

That one decision cost her $280,000. Her accountant found it 18 months after she opened. It was completely preventable.

The pre-launch window — the 12 to 18 months before a physician opens an independent clinic — is the most financially dangerous period of their career. Three predictable, preventable mistakes destroy financial outcomes for the majority:

Incorporation timing: the wrong entity structure costs $200,000 to $400,000 in tax losses over five years.

Credentialing sequence: a 90-day payer enrollment delay costs $100,000 to $450,000 in lost revenue.

Working capital: 92% of new clinics hit a cash crisis at months two to four.

The data point that frames everything: physicians who engage an external advisor launch on time and on budget at 84%, versus 42% for those who go it alone.

That gap is the business."

*[Pause. Advance to slide 2.]*

---

## SLIDE 2 — Solution (0:45–1:20)

"PhysicianLabs is the first fixed-curriculum, fixed-price pre-launch advisory program for independent physicians.

The $30,000 to $60,000 consulting firms that solve this problem today are real. They know exactly how to prevent the $280,000 mistake. But they are ad-hoc, relationship-dependent, and available only to the physicians who already know the right people.

PhysicianLabs is the TurboTax moment for physician practice formation. The same knowledge. Fixed curriculum. Fixed price. Accessible to any physician going independent — not just the ones with the right referral network.

Three tiers: a $500 self-serve toolkit, a $2,500 founder-guided 12-week program, and a $10,000 done-for-you launch.

The delivery is concierge in Year 1. That is intentional — the concierge is the product specification for the SaaS we build in Year 2."

*[Advance to slide 3.]*

---

## SLIDE 3 — Market + Why Now (1:20–1:50)

"5,000 to 10,000 new independent physician practice formations per year in the US. Each one spending $30,000 to $60,000 on unproductized startup consulting. TAM: $23 to $46 million annually in new-launch advisory alone.

The conventional narrative is that independent physician practice is dying. That's the incomplete version.

Independent ownership dropped from 60% to 42% since 2012 — acknowledge it. But the physicians choosing independence today are doing it deliberately. The Direct Primary Care market is $61 billion and growing at 10 to 15% annually. Nine new physician-ownership platforms launched in 2025 specifically to enable independent practice.

The era of physicians accidentally going independent is over. The era of physicians intentionally going independent — and needing expert infrastructure to do it right — is beginning."

*[Advance to slide 4.]*

---

## SLIDE 4 — Business Model + Traction (1:50–2:25)

"Year 1: $198,000. 65 physicians. Zero paid CAC — organic through specialty physician communities.

Blended ARPU: $4,600. LTV to CAC in Year 1: 23.5 to 1. Gross margin north of 80%.

The model evolves in three stages: concierge delivery in Year 1 validates and specifies the product. SaaS launches in Year 1 to 2. A marketplace of vetted specialists — accountants, attorneys, credentialing consultants — creates network effects from Year 2 onward.

A large incumbent can copy the checklist. They cannot copy the specialist referral network built relationship by relationship through each engagement.

No paid engagements yet. That changes in the next 30 days."

*[Advance to slide 5.]*

---

## SLIDE 5 — Team + Ask (2:25–3:00)

"Five years at McKinsey. Eleven years in product management at Amazon and AWS. A brother who is an orthopedic surgeon who has opened two independent clinic locations.

The McKinsey engagement model is structurally identical to a concierge clinic launch: understand a complex client situation, apply a structured framework, deliver recommendations that change the client's financial trajectory. That is not a background that needs to be learned for this role. It transfers directly.

From this panel, I am looking for three things: warm introductions to pre-launch physicians in your networks, design partner conversations, and advisory perspective on productizing a professional services concierge model at scale.

PhysicianLabs. The TurboTax moment for physician practice formation."

*[Hold eye contact. Do not add anything after the closing line.]*

## Q&A Prep (Most Likely Follow-Ons)

**"No paying customers — why should I believe physicians will pay?"**
> "You are right. No physician has paid yet. That is why the next 30 days have one job: close the first paid engagement. I have warm referrals being requested this week. The discovery calls are structured on Mom Test discipline — past behavior and real financial pain, not forward-looking purchase intent. If five discovery calls end without a paying customer, I will have specific, actionable data on why."

**"What stops Physicians Thrive from building this tomorrow?"**
> "They can copy the checklist content. They cannot copy two things: the structured product experience that makes it accessible at $500 to $10,000, and the specialist referral network. The network is built through personal relationships, one engagement at a time. It takes 18 to 24 months and cannot be purchased."

**"This sounds like consulting, not a startup."**
> "Concierge delivery is Phase 1, not the destination. The first 10 engagements tell us which of the 50 steps generate the most physician anxiety, which specialist introductions provide the most relief, and where the content gaps are. That data is the product specification for the SaaS. Building the SaaS before this data exists produces a generic product. Building it after produces a precise one."
