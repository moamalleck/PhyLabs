## 1. North Star Metric

**Recommended NSM: _Active paying practices that received a verified revenue-recovery finding in the last 30 days._**

Tested against Ellis's criteria:
| Criterion | Does the NSM meet it? |
|---|---|
| Reflects real customer value | ✅ A "verified recovery finding" *is* the value we sell |
| Leading indicator of revenue | ✅ Precedes PoC→Program conversion and subscription retention |
| The team can move it | ✅ Via acquisition, PoC delivery quality, and retention |
| Not a vanity metric | ✅ Signups/traffic are inputs *underneath* it, not the star |
| Simple, one number | ✅ A count of active, value-receiving practices |

**Why not the alternatives:** email signups / traffic (vanity, one-time), PoCs sold (one-time, ignores retention), or raw MRR (a *result*, not a *leading* value signal). The NSM deliberately couples **value delivered** with **retention**, which is what compounds into an investable story.

## 2. The growth equation (driver tree)

```
NSM (active value-receiving practices)
  = [ New PoCs sold ]
      × [ PoC → Program conversion ]        (model: 65%)
      × [ Program → active retention ]       (model churn: <2%/mo)
      + [ Expansion: founding $599 → standard $799 ]
```
Mapped to our existing AARRR funnel work:
- **Acquisition** → traffic → signups (the live channel test + the Revenue-Leak Scorecard).
- **Activation** → PoC delivers a verified finding (the NSM's numerator).
- **Revenue** → PoC→Program (65%) → subscription ($599 founding / $799 standard).
- **Retention** → keep churn <2%/mo.
- **Referral** → physician-refers-physician (compounds inside tight professional networks).

Balfour's order-of-operations: **retention and referral compound**, so protect churn and stand up a referral loop *early*, not after acquisition is "solved."

## 4. Goals as OKRs (ambition + rigor)

### OKR 1 — Prove the acquisition→PoC engine (Q3 2026, Months 1–3)
**Objective:** Turn the channel test + scorecard into the first paid PoCs and the first program customer.
- **KR1:** Sell **≥ 2 paid $1,000 PoCs** by end of Month 3 (Sep-26). *(model: 2 PoCs in Mo3)*
- **KR2:** Convert **≥ 1 PoC → Transition Program** (validates the 65% assumption at n=1). *(model: 1 program customer Mo3)*
- **KR3:** Ship the **Revenue-Leak Scorecard** and drive **≥ 1 of the three test channels to a cost-per-signup low enough to feed PoCs** (from the channel tracker).
- **KR4:** Keep peak cash need ≤ **$17.5K** (Month 3) funded; hit **cash-flow breakeven in Month 4**.

### OKR 2 — Build the retention + referral compounding (Months 4–6)
**Objective:** Make growth durable before scaling spend.
- **KR1:** Reach **6 cumulative program customers** by Month 6. *(model)*
- **KR2:** Hold **monthly subscription churn < 2%**. *(model assumption — now a target)*
- **KR3:** PoC→Program conversion **≥ 65%** sustained over the cohort.
- **KR4:** Launch a **physician-referral loop**; ≥ 20% of new PoCs referral-sourced.

### OKR 3 — Scale to the investable line (Months 7–12)
**Objective:** Reach a repeatable, retention-backed revenue base.
- **KR1:** **24 cumulative customers** and **MRR ≥ $16K** by Month 12. *(model)*
- **KR2:** **Net revenue retention > 100%** (founding $599 → standard $799 expansion + low churn).
- **KR3:** NSM ≥ **20 active value-receiving practices**.
- **KR4:** A **channel portfolio** live (no single channel > 60% of PoCs) — the Law-of-Shitty-Clickthroughs hedge.

**Cadence:** weekly review of the NSM + top 3 input drivers (new PoCs, PoC→Program %, churn) alongside the channel tracker; monthly re-forecast against the model.

## 5. Reconciling ambition vs. the model (the honest part)

- **The investor bar** ([investor-panel memory](../business-development/)) wants **~100% MoM early growth** and a **credible path to $100M**.
- **The model's early growth is genuinely steep but lumpy** (cohort-driven): MoM revenue jumps **+91% (Mo4→5), +93% (Mo7→8)** in program-onboarding months, then settles to single digits as it matures. It does **not** sustain 100% MoM — and pretending it does is exactly the gut-based goal-setting Balfour warns against.
- **How to hold both honestly:**
  1. **Base case = the model** ($1.13M / 46 customers / breakeven Mo4). Credible, fundable, defensible.
  2. **Stretch levers that could bend the curve above base:** the Revenue-Leak Scorecard (cheaper, higher-intent top-of-funnel), the referral loop (compounding), and a channel portfolio (offsets decay). These are *upside*, not baked into the base.
  3. **The $100M path is a later-stage story** — land the concierge wedge, then **expand into the standard SaaS tier ($799) and platform**. Concierge gets you to fundable traction; SaaS-scale economics get you to $100M. Don't conflate the two timelines in the pitch.
- **Because growth is lumpy, report the smooth signals investors trust:** NSM trend, **cohort retention**, PoC→Program conversion, and trailing-3-month revenue — not a single volatile MoM number.
