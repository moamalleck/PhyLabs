# Advisor-Fit Interview Guide & Scorecard
**Date:** 2026-06-25
**Project:** PhysicianLabs
**Purpose:** Run a consistent 15–20 min "advice call" with each Advisor Candidate to decide whether to *invite* them to a seat. The call is mutual exploration — you are interviewing them as much as they're sizing up you.
**Companion doc:** `docs/business-development/2026-06-25-advisor-target-tracker.md`

---

## Golden rules

- **Interview ≠ invitation.** Never offer the seat on the first call. Gather signal, then invite the best fit per seat in a separate follow-up with terms.
- **The best advisors prove value *on the call*** — they hand you a useful insight before any equity is discussed. Weight that heavily.
- **You want truth-tellers, not cheerleaders.** Optimize for people who'll disagree with you well.
- **Protect scarcity.** Three seats, one per category. More advisors ≠ better; engaged advisors = better.

---

## Call structure (15–20 min)

**0. Prep (2 min before):** Re-read their bio + the warm-intro context. Have one *real, current* problem ready to test how they think.

**1. Frame (1 min):**
> "Thanks for the time. I'm being deliberate about building a small advisory board — three people, one each from startup, healthcare, and go-to-market. This is really just to learn from you and see if there's mutual fit. No agenda beyond that."

**2. Core questions (10–12 min)** — pick 6–8 below.

**3. Live test (3–4 min):** Put a real decision in front of them (see seat-specific prompts). Listen for: do they ask sharp questions before answering? Is the advice specific and additive, or generic?

**4. Close (1–2 min):** Thank them, no commitment. *"This was genuinely useful — would it be alright if I followed up as I firm up the board?"* Note their energy on that ask — eagerness vs. politeness is signal.

---

## Core question bank (choose 6–8)

**Motivation & alignment**
1. "What makes you say yes to advising a company — and what's made you regret saying yes before?"
2. "Why this space / why us, specifically?"

**Additive value**
3. "If you were in my seat for the next 90 days, what would you be most worried about?"
4. "Where do you think founders like me most often get this wrong?"

**Engagement & availability (be concrete)**
5. "Realistically, what does good advising look like from you — cadence, format, responsiveness? How many other boards/companies are you advising right now?"
6. "When I hit a wall at 9pm, are you a 'text me' person or a 'schedule it' person?"

**Network / door-opening**
7. "Are there 2–3 specific people or doors you'd feel comfortable opening for us once you know us better?"

**Candor / chemistry**
8. "Tell me about a time you told a founder something they didn't want to hear. How'd it land?"

**Expectations**
9. "How do you usually like advisory relationships to be structured — time, term, and recognition?" *(Let them raise comp; don't lead with it.)*

## Seat-specific probes & live tests

### Startup seat (Cripe / Khera / Tullman)
- Probe: "What killed momentum at the stage we're at, in your experience?"
- Live test: *"We've got a paid PoC and 5 LOIs. Do I push for more PoCs now, or convert one LOI to a real contract first?"* — listen for prioritization logic, not a canned answer.

### Industry seat (Nabity / Dahle / Jerkins)
- Probe: "What do non-clinician founders consistently misunderstand about how physicians actually make money decisions?"
- Live test: *"What would make you, as a physician, trust a product like this enough to pay $599/mo?"* — listen for the real trust/credibility levers.

### Marketing/Sales seat (Gandolf / Kleine / fractional CMO)
- Probe: "What's the #1 mistake founders make marketing to physicians?"
- Live test: *"Given a concierge model and a $1,000 money-back PoC offer, where would you spend the first marketing dollar?"* — listen for channel specificity + understanding of physician buying behavior.

## Scorecard (score 1–5 each; weighted)

| Dimension | Weight | What a 5 looks like |
|---|---|---|
| **Additive domain fit** (seat-specific) | 25% | Expertise directly fills a real gap; not generalist |
| **Insight delivered on the call** | 20% | Gave you something genuinely useful before any ask |
| **Door-opening / network** | 15% | Named specific, relevant intros they'd make |
| **Engagement & realistic availability** | 15% | Concrete cadence; not overcommitted (≤ a few active boards) |
| **Candor & chemistry** | 15% | Will challenge you; you'd enjoy a hard conversation with them |
| **Motivation & integrity** | 10% | Clear, aligned "why"; mission > logo/equity collecting |

**Weighted score = Σ(score × weight).**
**Decision rule:** Invite if weighted score **≥ 4.0** *and* **zero red flags**. For each seat, invite only the single highest scorer; keep the runner-up warm.

## Red-flag screens (any one = pause)

- 🚩 Vague on availability — "happy to help anytime!" with no specifics.
- 🚩 Talks mostly about their own past wins; little curiosity about *your* business.
- 🚩 Leads with equity/comp before understanding the company.
- 🚩 Generic advice that could apply to any startup.
- 🚩 Overcommitted — advising 10+ companies; collects logos.
- 🚩 Can't name a single specific way they'd help or door they'd open.
- 🚩 Pushes their own service, fund, or vendors as the "solution."
- 🚩 No physician/healthcare nuance when the seat demands it.

---

## After the call

1. Score within 10 minutes while it's fresh; jot one sentence on gut feel.
2. Update the tracker Status Summary (Call Held → Verbal Yes).
3. Best scorer per seat → send the **invitation** (separate from this call) with proposed terms: time expectation (e.g., 1 hr/mo + ad-hoc), term (1 yr, renewable), and recognition (typically 0.1–0.25% advisory equity for early-stage, or a FAST-agreement tier). Keep the bar high — an empty seat beats a wrong advisor.
