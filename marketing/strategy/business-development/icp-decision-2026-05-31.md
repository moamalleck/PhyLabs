## ⏱️ Timing Refinement (added 2026-06-15, post-MVT)

The May 31 decision correctly identified the **profile** (hospital-to-independent / concierge physician with disposable income and high WTP). The first MVT and two independent unprompted interview signals (Samir May 28, Saf June 1) added the missing **timing** dimension:

> **The commercial buyer is the physician who has *just launched* (0–12 months post-launch) and is feeling the pain — not the pre-launch physician who has not yet felt it.**

Pre-launch physicians don't feel the pain and won't pay prospectively. They are now treated as a **nurture/education funnel** that converts *after* they launch. The profile from May 31 is unchanged; the entry *moment* shifts from "12–18 months before launch" to "0–12 months after launch," which is where the first paid commitment actually came from (Saf, post-launch ortho, confirmed June 6 — payment pending, family caveat).

**Validation status:** WTP directionally validated by one confirmed PoC commitment. **Hard validation gated on closing one non-family, card-charged PoC** (Matthew Tucci, Linda Xin, or Shaun Cowan).

## The Decision

**Primary ICP:** The hospital-employed physician making a deliberate transition to independent or concierge practice — with concierge medicine as the sharpest sub-segment.

**Secondary ICP:** Established independent physicians with 3+ income streams and no unified P&L visibility (Shaun Cowan profile). Queue 2–3 interviews before committing further resources here.

**Parked:** New grad PT/derm/chiro (original Jordan Park archetype). Not eliminated — but deprioritized. The pre-launch pain is real for this segment; the WTP is materially lower and the distribution is harder.

## Why This ICP, Why Now

The original "Jordan Park" archetype (new DPT grad, medical school debt, pre-launch) was built from desk research and one mentor session. The five customer interviews conducted since then have collectively pointed toward a different physician — one who is more financially established, more motivated, and significantly more willing to pay.

Four signals drove this decision:

**1. Sameer Gafoor's concierge recommendation (unprompted)**
> "I think you can go and do this in a very interesting way if you go to concierge doctors. That is your tap market... They are aggressively independent. They want to stay independent."
Sameer did not say this in response to a leading question. He said it after hearing the product concept and reflecting on who would actually pay.

**2. The Saf Malleck tax wallet moment**
Saf lost an estimated $200,000–$400,000 in preventable taxes before his accountant found the mistake — and he had been practicing independently for three years. He had disposable income, a clear understanding of what went wrong, and strong retrospective regret. The hospital-to-independent physician profile produces this situation at scale.

**3. Samir Master's WTP calibration**
> "People in my situation are likely generally exceedingly cheap."
Samir was describing independent practice owners broadly. But his caveat was about price sensitivity among physicians who are already established and budget-conscious. The hospital-to-independent physician, by contrast, is making a one-time, high-stakes transition decision and has a salary to draw on during the transition. The psychology and WTP are fundamentally different.

**4. The founder's own framing today**
> "my sell to that would be to doctors who already (a) have disposable income, (b) feel the pain and risk of setting things up poorly, (c) have a high willingness to pay for a frustration-free solution, (d) want to reclaim their agency from having worked at hospitals, and want to do so in an optimal manner that gives them crystal-clear clarity and visibility into their financials and efficiency metrics"

This is not a minor ICP tweak. It describes a different psychological profile, a different price point, and a different emotional hook.

## How the Core Product Changes (and Doesn't)

**What stays the same:**
- The 50-step launch checklist (the sequence still applies)
- The incorporation timing / financial structure module (even more critical — they have more to lose)
- Payer credentialing sequencing (still applies, though concierge/DPC has lower insurance complexity)
- Working capital planning (applies; they have more capital but the stakes are higher)

**What changes:**
- **Lead hook shifts:** From "don't make mistakes" (fear of the unknown) to "reclaim your agency with crystal-clear financials from Day 1" (aspiration + frustration relief)
- **Financial visibility is a core feature, not an add-on:** The P&L dashboard and efficiency metrics are part of the core promise, not a Year 2 upsell
- **The concierge pricing model module becomes a feature:** How to structure a membership-based practice (pricing the membership, patient panel sizing, payer elimination) is new content specifically for this ICP
- **Price point:** $2,500–$5,000 Launch Program is defensible; $10,000–$15,000 done-for-you is more accessible
- **Tone:** From "here is information you don't have" to "here is the operational clarity and financial independence you've been denied"

## Pricing Implication

The hospital-to-independent physician ICP justifies a significantly higher price point than the Jordan Park archetype:

| Tier | Old Price | New Price | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| Self-serve Launch Kit | $500 | $750–$1,000 | Higher-income ICP; price signal of quality |
| Launch Program (concierge) | $1,500–$3,000 | $3,500–$5,000 | Mid-career physician WTP; 12-week founder-guided + P&L setup |
| Full Launch (done-for-you) | $5,000–$15,000 | $8,000–$20,000 | High-earner specialist segment; full transition management |
| Active subscription | $200/mo | $500–$799/mo | Financial dashboard + efficiency metrics are core; not optional |

This price revision also addresses the mentor panel critique that the model is "too cheap to be taken seriously" by sophisticated physician buyers who use price as a quality signal.
