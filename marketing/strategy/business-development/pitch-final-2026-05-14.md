**From Saf's question:** The product was described at the category level ("launch system," "50-step guide") without naming what the physician actually receives. The revision names the product form (advisory program), the delivery format (founder-guided walkthrough), and the starting point (incorporation structure). A physician who hears the revised pitch should immediately understand: someone will sit with me on a weekly call and walk me through decisions I have not yet made.

**From Nishanth's question:** The MVP framing was invisible in the pitch. The revision makes the Wizard of Oz model a feature, not a gap: the first version is delivered by the founder personally, which means the product exists today and does not require a software build to generate value and revenue. The concreteness of "12-week program" signals a defined deliverable, not an open-ended consulting relationship.

**From Nigel's question:** The pitch was not raising HIPAA concerns -- but the word "clinic" combined with "compliance" was triggering the concern in technically sophisticated listeners. The revision explicitly locates the product in the business formation layer, before clinical operations begin. The physician's incorporation structure, payer credentialing timeline, and working capital plan do not involve patient data. Naming this preempts the question.

**From Rohan's question:** The buyer/user split question is the most structural critique. In most healthcare products, the physician uses the product but the institution buys it. PhysicianLabs is different because of timing: at the pre-launch moment, the institution does not exist yet. The physician IS the practice, the budget, and the decision maker simultaneously. The revision makes this explicit in the UVP rather than leaving it implied.

## The Business Model

### Model Selected: Pre-Launch Advisory Program (Concierge-First, SaaS-Second)

This is confirmed as the right starting model based on the founder-market fit analysis (McKinsey advisory background, physician network, energized by direct problem-solving) and the critique (the fastest path to the first paid transaction, the lowest risk of regulatory exposure, and the highest founder credibility signal to the physician buyer).

---

### Three Named Product Tiers

**Tier 1: PhysicianLabs Launch Kit -- $500 one-time**

A self-serve digital toolkit for the physician who has a business-savvy colleague and needs the map, not the guide. Includes: the 50-step state-specific checklist (WA State at launch), the financial structure module (when to incorporate, entity type selection, deductible expense categories, income splitting), the payer credentialing timeline (pre-populated for WA State's top 10 payers), and the working capital planner (parameterized by specialty, patient volume, and payer mix). Delivered as a structured digital document set. No calls, no guidance, no specialist introductions.

*Who buys it:* Physicians with a business-savvy colleague who need the structured framework but not the advisory relationship.

---

**Tier 2: PhysicianLabs Launch Program -- $2,000 to $3,000 one-time, then $200/month**

A 12-week, founder-guided weekly call program covering all 50 steps. Each week covers a defined set of decisions with pre-work, structured discussion, and next actions. Includes warm introductions to the specialist accountant (for incorporation and tax structure), the compliance attorney (for entity formation and state licensing), and the credentialing consultant (for payer enrollment sequencing) at the right step in the program. The $200/month subscription begins when the clinic opens and covers: credentialing deadline alerts, monthly benchmarking report (practice metrics vs. specialty peers using CMS public data), and access to the specialist referral network for ongoing needs.

*Who buys it:* Physicians going it entirely alone who need guidance and accountability, not just a checklist. The primary customer for Year 1.

*Retention argument for the $200/month subscription:* After the clinic opens, the subscription delivers three things the physician still needs: deadline alerts for annual credentialing renewals and payer re-enrollment cycles, the monthly benchmarking report (episode completion rate, denial rate, average collection time vs. specialty peers -- a viral sharing asset in physician communities), and on-demand access to the specialist network for questions that arise in the first year of operation. This is not the acute launch pain. It is the ongoing operational support that prevents the second wave of mistakes.

---

**Tier 3: PhysicianLabs Full Launch -- $5,000 to $15,000 one-time, then $200/month**

Full white-glove launch management for high-earning specialists who want to delegate the entire setup. Includes all Tier 2 deliverables plus: entity formation handled directly (PLLC or PC filing, registered agent, EIN, state licensing applications), credentialing submissions managed on the physician's behalf (payer applications, CAQH profile, Medicare/Medicaid enrollment), billing configuration support (EHR selection, NPI linkage, clearinghouse setup), and staffing support (medical secretary job brief, screening criteria, shortlist of candidates). The physician makes clinical decisions. PhysicianLabs handles the business.

*Who buys it:* Dermatologists, surgical specialists, and other high-billing-rate physicians whose hourly clinical value makes spending 40 hours on entity formation economically irrational. The done-for-you fee is framed as: two days of billings pays for the entire clinic setup.

### Why This Model is Most Suitable

**It generates revenue before it generates software.** The first Launch Program engagement requires no engineering, no product build, and no external funding. The founder delivers value through structured calls and specialist introductions using tools that already exist. The first paid transaction is achievable in 14 to 30 days.

**It validates before it productizes.** The first 5 to 10 Launch Program engagements reveal exactly which of the 50 steps generate the most anxiety, which specialist introductions provide the most relief, and which content gaps exist in the current framework. This is the product specification for the software platform. Building the SaaS product before this data exists produces a generic product. Building it after produces a precise one.

**It matches the founder's unfair advantage.** Five years of McKinsey advisory engagement experience, eleven years of Amazon and AWS product management, a physician family network, and a primary energy source in direct human problem-solving are all assets that compound in the concierge model and are largely irrelevant in a software-only model.

**It creates the specialist network that becomes the long-term moat.** Every specialist accountant, compliance attorney, and credentialing consultant introduced through a Launch Program engagement is a vetted supply-side candidate for the eventual marketplace layer (Model 3). The network cannot be purchased or rushed. It is built through relationships, one engagement at a time. Starting with the concierge model is the only way to build it at the pace and quality required.
