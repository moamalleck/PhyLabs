### Rank 1: H2 -- The Practice Launch Gauntlet (sharpened)

**Problem:** Physicians who decide to go independent do not know the 50+ operational, legal, financial, and compliance steps required to launch a viable practice. The financial structure mistakes alone -- incorporating too late, choosing the wrong entity type, not knowing deductible expense categories -- cost physicians hundreds of thousands of dollars in unnecessary taxes over the first five years. The operational mistakes -- missing credentialing deadlines, undercapitalizing for the 30 to 90 day payment lag, not knowing how to find a trained medical secretary -- determine whether the practice survives the first year.

**Market potential:**
- TAM: ~$796M (165,751 physician-owned US practices at $4,800/yr ARPU)
- SAM: ~100,000 practices (1 to 5 physicians, fee-for-service, EHR-equipped)
- ~20,000 new independent clinics open per year in the US
- Revenue model: $500 Launch Pack + $200/month active = $4,700 LTV (12-month average)
- LTV:CAC ratio modeled at 23.5:1 (community-led acquisition, zero paid CAC in Year 1)
- Practice management software growing at 8.96% to 10.54% CAGR for small practices

**Feasibility: HIGH**
- Wizard of Oz delivery at MVP: founder personally guides the first 3 to 5 design partners through the 50 steps
- No engineering required at launch (checklist, credentialing calendar, Google Sheet cash flow model)
- Subject matter experts available: accountants specializing in physician professional corporations (Chelsea's network contacts), compliance attorneys, FI mentor network
- Chelsea Parkman offered to connect Ash to her business coaching network with healthcare experience
- Ash's family network (Saf, Shaun) provides warm referral channels into physician communities

**Mentor feedback -- HIGH SIGNAL, TWO CONVERGING SOURCES:**

> David Jorjani (April 28): "Find the single moment when a doctor says 'This is hard, I wish it was easier' and would open their wallet. That trigger is what will clarify how to talk about the problem and what to build."

> David Jorjani (April 28): Business model sketch -- "Help a doctor set up a clinic for less than the prevailing market cost (e.g., $100K market rate, position at $60K through specialization) and capture the delta as margin."

> Chelsea Parkman (May 7): Immediate strong positive reaction. Coined "business in a box." Confirmed two-tier structure: self-serve guided tracker + premium white-glove compliance support.

> Chelsea Parkman (May 7): "Compliance is a high-value wedge. Doctors really do not want to handle compliance themselves, often do not know all the requirements, and fear the consequences of getting it wrong."

**Wallet moment -- clearest of any interview to date (Saf Malleck, May 11):**

> "The amount of tax that I'm paying is nauseating."

> "Had I known this five years ago, the house I'd be living in would be double the size -- just, it's stupid, like the amount of tax. We're all broke right now."

> "You're not taught and nobody really explains it to you... unless you have a good accountant."

Saf incorporated in July 2025 but started his practice in 2022. Three years of personal income tax rates on practice revenue instead of professional corporation rates. Estimated loss: hundreds of thousands of dollars. The fix required finding the right accountant by accident.

This is the clearest wallet moment from any interview in the dataset. It meets every Mom Test criterion: past behavior (paid wrong taxes for 3 years), real money lost, emotionally charged language unprompted, and a retrospective regret signal ("Had I known this five years ago").

### New Hypothesis Formulated from Interview Evidence

#### H6: Physician Financial Structure -- Incorporation Timing and Tax Optimization

**Source:** Saf Malleck interview (May 11). Cross-pattern also consistent with multi-stream revenue visibility from Shaun Cowan.

**Problem:** Independent physicians lose significant wealth in the first 3 to 7 years of practice because no one proactively advises them on:

1. When to incorporate (professional corporation in Canada; PLLC, PC, or S-Corp election in the US) -- delaying costs hundreds of thousands in unnecessary taxes
2. How to structure income splitting through a professional corporation
3. Which expense categories are deductible under a medical professional corporation vs. personal income
4. How to use retained earnings inside the corporation to invest and compound tax-sheltered

This is not a software problem at MVP. It is an advisory and information gap that manifests as a catastrophic financial loss spread over years -- invisible until a good accountant surfaces it by accident.

**Market potential:**
- Every independent physician who incorporates without proactive guidance loses money on this
- The financial advisory market for medical professionals is fragmented, expensive, and reactive
- No product or service is positioned as "the proactive financial structure guide for new independent physicians"
- As a feature within H2 (Launch Gauntlet), this is the highest-value single step in the checklist -- the one that has the largest long-term financial impact

**Feasibility: HIGH** as a content layer within H2. The MVP is: a module inside the Launch Checklist specifically about when and how to set up the professional corporation structure, with referrals to accountants who specialize in physician professional corporations.

**Verdict:** This is not a standalone hypothesis -- it is the sharpest, most emotionally resonant feature of H2. It should be the lead content element in the Launch Checklist product because it is the pain point with the most documented wallet moment. Position it as: "The mistake that costs independent physicians $200,000 to $400,000 before they realize it happened."
