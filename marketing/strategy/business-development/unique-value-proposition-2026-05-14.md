## The One Problem (Summary)

Independent physicians who decide to go independent lose hundreds of thousands of dollars and sometimes their entire practice in the first three to five years -- not because they are bad doctors, but because no one tells them the financial, legal, and operational structure they need to have in place from Day One.

The sharpest form of this pain: physicians who delay incorporation lose $200,000 to $400,000 in preventable taxes before a good accountant surfaces the mistake, usually years after it began. This is the wallet moment the product is built around.

## Key Features and How They Address the Problem

### Feature 1: Financial Structure Module -- Lead Element

**What it does:** Guides the physician through the single most expensive set of decisions in the pre-launch checklist: when to incorporate (professional corporation in Canada; PLLC, PC, or S-Corp in the US), how to structure income splitting, which expense categories are deductible, and how to use retained earnings to compound tax-sheltered.

**Problem addressed:** Physicians who incorporate late -- or never incorporate -- pay personal income tax rates on practice revenue. The estimated cost of this mistake over three to five years is $200,000 to $400,000. No competitor, EHR vendor, or standard financial advisor proactively surfaces this before it begins.

**Why no competitor has this:** It requires specialized knowledge of medical professional corporations, not generic business formation. The market for this advice is fragmented, expensive, and reactive. PhysicianLabs positions it as the lead content -- delivered proactively, at the pre-launch moment.

---

### Feature 2: 50-Step Launch Checklist and Tracker (state-specific)

**What it does:** A structured, deadline-linked checklist of every legal, credentialing, billing, financial, and patient acquisition step required to open an independent clinic. WA State at launch (Ontario, Canada in Phase 2). Each step includes a plain-language explanation of what to do, why it matters, and what goes wrong if it is skipped.

**Problem addressed:** The information vacuum. Physicians are trained to practice medicine, not to operate a business. There is no curriculum, no guide, and no product that maps the full 50-step sequence. The workaround is "know a business-savvy colleague" -- which Saf's interview confirmed is the only current solution. PhysicianLabs is the product for when that colleague does not exist in your network.

**Why no competitor has this:** Practice management tools assume the practice is already operating. They have no pre-launch state. The 50-step checklist is not a feature that can be retrofitted into athenahealth or Tebra -- it requires being present at the decision moment, before the physician has committed to any vendor.

---

### Feature 3: Payer Credentialing Timeline Manager

**What it does:** A calendar-driven tracker that shows each payer's application status, the 90 to 120 day processing window, and deadline alerts when the credentialing sequence is at risk. Pre-populated for WA State's top 10 payers (Regence, Premera, Aetna, UHC, Cigna, L and I, Medicaid, Medicare, BCBS, First Choice).

**Problem addressed:** 65% of new clinics open without full payer enrollment. The root cause is not negligence -- it is not knowing that credentialing takes 90 to 120 days and must begin immediately after entity formation, not at clinic opening. This is the single most preventable cause of revenue delay in the first year.

**Why no competitor has this:** Credentialing management tools exist for established practices. None are designed for the physician who has not yet submitted their first application.

---

### Feature 4: Working Capital and Cash Flow Planner

**What it does:** A parameterized projection model that calculates the cash gap between first patient visit and first insurance payment (30 to 90 days), estimates operating capital required for three to six months at projected volume, and flags which credentialing approvals must clear first to minimize the gap.

**Problem addressed:** 92% of new independent clinics hit a cash crisis at months two to four. The cause is structural: the physician has expenses from day one (rent, salary, insurance) but no insurance revenue until credentialing clears. Without a model, physicians undercapitalize. With the model, the gap is visible before the clinic opens.

**Why no competitor has this:** The same structural reason. Pre-launch financial planning for physicians is not a category that exists in software form.

---

### Feature 5: Concierge Delivery Tier (Business Doula)

**What it does:** A founder-led guided walkthrough of all 50 steps, with referrals to specialist accountants for incorporation setup, credentialing consultants for payer enrollment, and compliance attorneys for entity formation. Three tiers: $500 self-serve (map only), $1,500 to $3,000 concierge (guided walkthrough + specialist referrals), $5,000 to $15,000 done-for-you (full white-glove launch management).

**Problem addressed:** Some physicians need a map. Others need someone to walk with them. The done-for-you tier is designed for high-earner specialists (derm, surgical) who want to delegate the entire setup. Chelsea Parkman's validation: "Compliance is a high-value wedge. Doctors really do not want to handle compliance themselves."

**Why no competitor has this:** Healthcare practice consultants exist but are not productized, not specialty-specific, and not positioned at the pre-launch moment. This is an unoccupied category.
