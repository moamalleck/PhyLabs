## Part 0: Bottom Line Up Front

The first MVT (Minimum Viable Test) was a **paid-offer experiment**: would a physician pay real money for founder-guided advisory *before* the product exists? The literal hypothesis (a pre-launch PT in Washington pays $500+ within 7 days) was **not cleanly tested and did not pass on its own terms** by the June 4 deadline. But the experiment succeeded at its deeper job: it told us *who actually pays and why*.

When the explicit paid offer was finally made (a $1,000 money-back "we find $20K or it's free" Proof-of-Concept session) to a physician who has **already launched and feels the pain**, it converted to a confirmed commitment (Saf Malleck, June 6). That single conversion, plus two independent unprompted interview signals, validates a clear pivot:

- **The buyer is the just-launched physician (0–12 months post-launch), not the pre-launch physician.**
- **The winning offer is a money-back, ROI-quantified PoC — not a generic program subscription.**
- **The value frame that opens the wallet is savings-first ("save $100K/year") — not features.**

Two honest caveats keep this from being a clean "PASS": the one conversion is the **founder's brother** (weak external validity), and **no card has been charged yet** (the session is booked but payment is pending). The next 14 days must close a *non-family, card-charged* PoC to convert this from a strong signal into hard validation.

## Part 2: MVT Verdict

### 2.1 Against the literal hypothesis: **INCONCLUSIVE / NOT PASS**

- The $750 offer was **never delivered to 5 warm pre-launch PTs** as designed. The Reddit prospects did not reply; the LinkedIn Tier 1/2 list did not convert; no pre-launch PT in WA was actually asked to pay.
- By the strict bar ("1+ card charged by June 4"), the count on June 4 was **zero**. On the original framing this is, at best, a *SIGNAL — NOT PASS* and arguably a soft FAIL on the pre-launch PT segment.

### 2.2 Against the riskiest assumption: **DISAMBIGUATED — and this is the win**

The experiment was explicitly designed to distinguish:
- **Explanation A (timing/framing):** no one paid because the ask was always framed as free/design-partner, never as a real paid offer.
- **Explanation B (structural):** physicians genuinely won't pay before clinic revenue, regardless of framing.

The result splits the difference in a productive way: **Explanation B is true for pre-launch physicians, and Explanation A was true for the post-launch ones.** Pre-launch physicians don't feel the pain, so they won't pay prospectively (confirmed by Samir *and* Saf, unprompted). But a *post-launch* physician who feels the pain, given an explicit money-back ROI offer, **did** commit. The wallet opens when (a) the pain is felt and (b) the offer carries quantified, guaranteed ROI.

### 2.3 Overall: **STRATEGIC SUCCESS via validated pivot**

The MVT failed its literal hypothesis but succeeded at its real purpose: it produced a validated repointing of ICP, offer, and message, plus the first paid commitment in the company's history. That is the correct outcome for a first MVT — it killed the wrong target and revealed the right one.

### 2.4 The two caveats that gate the claim

1. **Family bias.** The one conversion is the founder's brother. The experiment design itself flags this: "Validate WTP signals against non-family subjects before treating them as market-representative." A brother saying yes to a brother is the weakest form of external validity.
2. **No card charged yet.** "Paid PoC — confirmed" is a written commitment, not collected revenue. The session is to be booked by June 13; "First $1K paid" is still an open Week-2 milestone (June 20). Until the card is charged, this is the strongest *signal* to date, not closed validation.

## Part 3: Customer Interview Synthesis (5 physicians, 7 sessions)

| Subject | Role | ICP fit | Headline contribution |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hannan Qureshi, MD | ENT, Atlanta | Partial | Patient acquisition channels; PE squeeze dynamics |
| Shaun Cowan, MD | Surgeon/ICU, Alberta | Partial (secondary ICP) | Multi-income-stream P&L blindness; "$799 feels high" |
| Saf Malleck, MD ×2 | Ortho, Ontario | Strong | Setup pain, "nauseating" tax, debt-first myth, WTP $1K/$10K, **first paid commitment** |
| Sameer Gafoor, MD | Interventional Cardiology, Seattle | Counter-persona | Recommended **concierge** as the tap market, unprompted |
| Samir Master, MD MBA | Derm, Bellevue | Strong (but competitor) | $115 vs $290 squeeze, $200K/yr admin, **building a competing product** |

### 3.1 Confirmed across two or more independent sources (high confidence)

1. **The setup information vacuum is real.** Nobody teaches physicians to run a business (Saf, Shaun, Samir, Hannan).
2. **Pre-launch physicians don't feel the pain; just-launched do.** Validated *independently and unprompted* by Samir (May 28) and Saf (June 1) — the single most important cross-interview finding, and the spine of the pivot.
3. **Admin overhead is enormous.** Samir: $200K/yr; Saf: accountant every second Saturday, still unresolved.
4. **Payer discrimination against independents is systemic.** Samir: $115 (independent) vs $290 (hospital) for the same Blue Cross visit.
5. **EMRs are entrenched and inadequate.** Samir: ModMed graded D- to C over 16 years; Saf: Acuro chosen by familiarity, not quality.
6. **The financial taboo is cultural, not accidental.** Saf: a financial lens is seen as conflicting with "medicine is not for money" — which is *why* the knowledge never transfers.

### 3.2 Single-source signals (need a second confirmation)

- Secretary hiring as the #1 setup pain (Saf only — 2,000–4,000 applicants).
- Concierge medicine as the sharpest ICP (Sameer only).
- IPAs (Pro Alliance, WWMG) as a distribution channel (Samir only).
- Facebook private doctor groups as distribution (Saf only).

### 3.3 The willingness-to-pay picture

| Frame | Amount | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Retrospective (knowing the outcome) | $10,000 | Saf |
| Prospective (before feeling pain) | $1,000 | Saf |
| Value-unlock framing | "save $100K/year minimum" | Saf |
| Segment ceiling caveat | "doctors don't want to pay anything" | Saf + Samir ("exceedingly cheap") |
| Price resistance at subscription | "$799 feels slightly high" | Shaun |

The WTP story is coherent: physicians are cheap *prospectively* and generous *retrospectively*; the job of the offer is to collapse that gap by proving savings up front. The $1,000 money-back PoC does exactly this.

### 3.4 Competitive intelligence

Samir Master, MD MBA is **building a direct competitor** ("Rippling meets Compass for outpatient medicine"): PRD complete, 25 TDDs, 100+ mapped workflows, one developer, a network of 100+ derm practices. He is single-specialty, part-time, and building for his own practice first. He is best treated as market validation from inside the ICP and a possible future partner/customer — not dismissed, not feared.

## Part 4: The Decision — Validated Pivot

| Lever | Before (as of May 28) | After (validated June 15) |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary ICP timing** | Pre-launch physician | **Just-launched physician (0–12 months post-launch)** who feels acute pain; pre-launch becomes a nurture/education funnel that converts *after* launch |
| **ICP profile** | Hospital-to-independent / concierge (May 31 decision) | Unchanged and reinforced — but sharpened to the *moment* of acute pain (the 6–12 months after opening) |
| **Lead offer** | $750 12-week generic program | **$1,000 money-back PoC: "we find $20K you're not collecting, or it's free"** → locked $599/mo founder rate → platform |
| **Message** | Fear of the unknown mistake | **ROI-first: quantified savings before features** |
| **Validation status** | WTP unvalidated | WTP **directionally validated** (1 confirmed commitment); **hard validation gated on one non-family, card-charged PoC** |
