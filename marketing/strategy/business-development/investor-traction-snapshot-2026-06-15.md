**Purpose:** Not a pitch. Evidence that makes an investor lean in or refer someone who should.

## THE 3-MINUTE TALK TRACK

*Practice this until it runs 2:45. The 15-second buffer is for the room.*

---

### [0:00–0:30] — The discovery arc

"Over the last six weeks I've had seven structured customer discovery sessions with five independent physicians — in Ontario, Alberta, Washington, and Georgia. I'm going to tell you what I ran, what came back, and what I changed because of it."

"I started with a hypothesis: physicians finishing medical training who are about to launch their first independent clinic will pay $500 for a structured launch program before they open. I was wrong. And finding out I was wrong is the most important thing that's happened in this company."

---

### [0:30–1:10] — One doctor's story (the pain spine)

"My brother Saf is an orthopedic surgeon. He opened his second independent clinic in January of this year. On our last call I asked him directly: 'What would you have paid for someone to guide your incorporation and tax structure?'"

"He said: '$10,000. In retrospect.'"

"'What would you have paid before you knew the pain?'"

"'$1,000. Maybe. But the only way you get people to pay is if you show them they'll save $100,000 a year minimum. That changes the math.'"

"Then he told me why. He'd been incorporating in the wrong sequence — paying personal income tax on money he could have run through his corporation first. He and his wife are in significant debt. Not because the practice is failing. Because nobody ever told him the sequence. No professor. No residency. No bank. Not even his accountant — until it was too late."

"I've now heard this same story, in different forms, from every physician I've talked to."

---

### [1:10–1:40] — The data points that prove it's not just one family

"Here are three numbers from outside my family:"

"**$115 versus $290.** That's the Blue Cross reimbursement for the exact same new patient visit — one at an independent derm practice, one at Swedish Hospital. Same doctor. Same procedure. $175 gap per patient, systematically. That's from Samir Master, a 16-year independent derm practice owner in Bellevue who has a Kellogg MBA and sat on the Netflix board. He called it 'systematic collusion against small practices.'"

"**$200,000 per year.** That's Samir's annual admin overhead — five people answering phones, two billing, three medical assistants — driven entirely by point solutions that don't integrate. And he grades his EMR, which he's been using for 16 years, as a C-minus."

"**60% to 42%.** That's the share of US physicians in independent practice since 2012. Declining market — but the physicians *choosing* independence today are doing it with conviction. That's who we're building for."

---

### [1:40–2:10] — The pivot that came from the data

"Two independent physician interviews — not connected to each other — told me the same thing without me asking: 'Pre-launch physicians don't feel the pain. They only know they need help after they've launched and the mistakes have already been made.'"

"So I pivoted. The ICP is no longer the physician preparing to launch. It's the physician who just launched — zero to twelve months in — and is currently absorbing the cost of not knowing what they didn't know. And the ICP profile shifted: from new grad PTs with medical school debt to mid-career, hospital-employed physicians making a deliberate move to independent or concierge practice. They have income, they understand what structural mistakes cost, and they are paying for financial clarity — not a checklist."

"The offer changed too. It's not a $750 subscription. It's a $1,000 Proof-of-Concept session: we go through your incorporation structure and your P&L in 90 minutes. If we don't find you at least $20,000 in improvements, I give the $1,000 back. The wallet opens when the ROI is guaranteed up front."

---

### [2:10–2:30] — Revenue locked

"I have one paid commitment. $1,000. It's from my brother — and I'm going to own that."

"He's the first person who has said yes to a real dollar number with real terms. The session is booked. The money has not been collected yet. And the next thing I need to prove is that I can get that same yes from someone I'm not related to."

"I have three physicians with prototypes in hand right now — Shaun Cowan, Hannan Qureshi, and another ortho surgeon Saf referred me to. One of them pays in the next 30 days, or I go back and find out what's wrong with the offer."

---

### [2:30–3:00] — What I haven't proven and the plan to prove it

"Two things I have not yet proven, and I want to be specific about how I'll validate them:"

"**First: a non-family physician will pay.** The 30-day target is one card charged from someone I'm not related to. I have three names. If none of them close, I'll know either the offer is wrong or the profile is wrong — and I'll run a second experiment with a tighter brief."

"**Second: I have a repeatable acquisition channel.** Every conversation I've had came through my personal network. I don't know yet if AAPP, DPC Frontier, or the whitecoatinvestor community converts. The 60-day goal: one piece of value-lead content in a physician community that generates five or more inbound DMs. That tells me whether I can acquire customers I've never met."

"If both of those prove out in 60 days, I have a business. If one of them doesn't, I know exactly what needs to change."

## Q&A PREP CARD

*Questions likely to come up. Have a tight answer for each.*

---

**"What's your revenue today?"**

> "One confirmed commitment at $1,000. Uncollected. It's my brother. I'm not going to dress that up — it's the earliest signal, not a revenue number. The next 30 days are about closing the same offer with a non-family physician."

---

**"You said you pivoted your ICP. How many times have you changed direction?"**

> "Once — and it was data-driven. I started with pre-launch new grad physicians. Two independent interviews told me, unprompted, that pre-launch physicians don't feel the pain and won't pay. I changed the ICP timing from pre-launch to post-launch, and the profile from new grads to mid-career hospital-to-independent physicians. The core product — helping physicians structure their financial and operational setup correctly — didn't change. The entry moment did."

---

**"What's the competitive threat? Who else is solving this?"**

> "The most interesting thing I know about competition: the most credentialed independent physician I interviewed — Kellogg MBA, Kleiner Perkins, Netflix board — is building the same product himself. That's market validation. His product is operational, post-launch. Mine starts at the transition decision, 12 to 18 months earlier. The window before the clinic opens is structurally unattractive to operational software vendors — their revenue model begins after you've opened. That pre-launch and just-launched window is where I own."

---

**"What does the $115 versus $290 reimbursement gap have to do with your product?"**

> "It's the structural squeeze that makes the business fragile from day one. Independent physicians are getting paid 40% of what a hospital physician gets for the same patient. If you don't have your financial structure optimized — incorporated correctly, billing cleanly, overhead controlled — you're already losing before you treat anyone. That's what PhysicianLabs solves."

---

**"You haven't mentioned a product. What are you actually selling right now?"**

> "Right now I'm selling 90 minutes of the founder's time and a money-back guarantee. That's the PoC. If I find at least $20,000 in financial improvements for a physician in that session, I've proved the value of the full platform. The first two or three of those sessions are how I build the product spec — not from assumptions, from the specific things physicians needed in those sessions."

---

**"What do you need from us, specifically?"**

> "Two things. First, if you know a physician who opened an independent or concierge practice in the last 12 months and is still figuring out their financial structure — that's an intro worth more to me than almost anything right now. Second, if this thesis is interesting to you at all, I'd like to stay in contact as the 30-day data comes in. I'll know in a month whether this offer closes with non-family physicians."
