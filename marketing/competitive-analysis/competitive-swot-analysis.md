## Executive Summary

### The One Insight That Overrides Everything Else

Every major competitor in the practice management and EHR space — from $30K/year Epic to $49/month SimplePractice — **treats the patient as a passive record to be managed, not an active relationship to be retained**. Not a single competitor has a retention-first patient engagement product built for the plan-of-care completion problem.

This is not a minor gap. It is a $162,000-per-year-per-practice bleeding wound that is currently invisible to every software vendor in the space.

PhysicianLabs' wedge is not a feature. It is a complete repositioning of what practice management software is *for*.

## Cross-Competitor Gap Analysis

### The Three Structural Gaps No Competitor Addresses

#### Gap 1: Plan-of-Care Completion / Patient Retention
**The problem:** 70% of PT patients (WebPT 2025 State of Rehab Therapy) do not complete their authorized plan of care. The same pattern applies to chiropractic (patients stop after acute pain resolves, before the corrective care phase). Average cost per independent practice: **~$162,000/year in lost revenue**.

**What every competitor does:** Scheduling, billing, documentation, and basic appointment reminders. Zero competitors have a product built around *keeping patients on their plan of care*.

**What exists in adjacent space:** Patient engagement platforms like Luma Health and Klara offer messaging, but they are communication tools — not retention-outcome tools.

**What PhysicianLabs can build:** A retention engine that identifies patients at drop-off risk before they ghost, delivers personalized re-engagement sequences (by clinical milestone, not by appointment slot), tracks plan-of-care completion rates as a KPI, and shows the practice a monthly "retained revenue" number. This is a category creation move, not a feature war.

#### Gap 2: The New Clinic Launcher Segment
**The problem:** No competitor sells to physicians who haven't opened yet. Every product requires an existing patient panel, existing billing data, or existing operations. The pre-launch physician is invisible to the entire market.

**The data:** 57.8% of physicians now work for hospitals or corporate groups (AMA 2024). The physicians who choose to go independent are a self-selected group of high-intent entrepreneurs. They are actively searching for tools *before* launch — and finding nothing designed for them.

**What every competitor does:** Onboard existing practices. No one builds for Day 0.

**What PhysicianLabs can build:** A milestone-based launch system ($500 Launch Pack → $200/month) that guides a physician from LLC formation through credentialing, first patient intake, and first insurance claim — with the full platform ready on the day they open. This is a category that does not exist.

#### Gap 3: Integration-Layer Architecture (No Migration Required)
**The problem:** Every competitor requires EHR migration. This is the #1 objection in every enterprise software sale in healthcare. Physicians delay, defer, or reject purchases because the switching cost is perceived as catastrophic.

**What every competitor does:** Sell a replacement system and manage the migration.

**What PhysicianLabs can build:** An integration layer that plugs on top of the physician's existing EHR (Epic Community Connect, athenahealth, Tebra) and adds the retention, analytics, and launch-management capabilities that the EHR doesn't provide. **The migration is eliminated as an objection because the migration doesn't happen.**

| Criterion | Assessment |

| **Competitive whitespace** | Zero competitors address this. Not a feature gap — a category gap |

**The critical reframe for PhysicianLabs:**

Samir's product and PhysicianLabs are **not competing for the same customer at the same moment.**

| Dimension | Samir Master's Product | PhysicianLabs |
|---|---|---|
| Customer timing | Established, running practice (post-launch) | Physician in transition — hospital-to-independent/concierge (pre-launch) |

**Samir's own words on the pre-launch gap:** He explicitly critiqued the pre-launch distribution model (500 UW grads across 30 departments) as "structurally hard." **What Samir's existence confirms:** The market for physician practice intelligence is real, under-served, and being pursued by high-caliber domain experts. The beachhead opportunity is not in Samir's territory (established multi-physician derm practices); it is in the transition moment he explicitly said is hard to reach.

**Monitoring required:** If Samir ships and the two large derm groups adopt, watch whether his product extends into the pre-launch or physician-transition use case. Current assessment: unlikely in 12–18 months given scope and build complexity.

**Gap for PhysicianLabs:** Samir's product does not serve the hospital-to-independent/concierge physician in transition. It does not cover incorporation timing, credentialing sequencing for a new practice, working capital planning, or the employed-to-independent financial structure. The pre-launch window is structurally outside his architecture.
